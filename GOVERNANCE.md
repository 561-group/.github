## Indigenous data: the CARE Principles

Some of the 561 Group's work concerns Indigenous peoples, languages and knowledge, and its research is grounded in
Lakota relational thought. The Group follows the [CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance](https://www.gida-global.org/care)
of the Global Indigenous Data Alliance:

- **Collective benefit.** Data ecosystems are designed and function in ways that let Indigenous peoples benefit from the data.
- **Authority to control.** Indigenous peoples' rights and interests in Indigenous data are recognised, and their authority to control such data is empowered.
- **Responsibility.** Those working with Indigenous data are accountable for how its use supports Indigenous peoples' self-determination and collective benefit.
- **Ethics.** Indigenous peoples' rights and wellbeing are the primary concern at all stages of the data life cycle.

What this means here:

- Open licenses on these repositories cover the 561 Group's own software and writing. They grant no rights over
  Indigenous cultural or traditional knowledge, which stays under the authority of the people it comes from.
- Lakota cultural material is published, if at all, under the 561 Group Repository License, whose section 3 reserves
  the authority of the tiyóšpaye; some of it is not offered for circulation at all.
