# Underwrite the parts of the future that should stay free

*A public invitation to curious patrons, practical sponsors, independent funders, and the people who know them.*

A venture-backed company raises money by selling a piece of itself, or a promise about future economic value.

We make some of our work available for anyone to inspect, reproduce, criticize, fork, and improve. We want those practices to get **more** abundant as people fund them—not less.

The 561 Group is an unusual science-to-engineering-to-operations-to-public-services organization. You can [read the introductory field guide](OPEN-WORK.md), but we'd rather you start with one artifact below. The propositions are interesting only if the sources withstand examination.

Our invitation: **become the person who helped something useful and gloriously odd become real without enclosing it.**

## Pick a frontier, not a vanity tier

These are genuine *directions* anchored in existing public source. None is a prepaid service contract, fixed price quote, reserved technical deliverable, proof of a deployed product, or claim that anyone has funded us.

### Public scientific instruments for agent attention and work

What would it take for human and machine coworkers to say what consumed a scarce resource, why, and whether the result was worthwhile?

[Start with the public attention-allocation readout](https://github.com/lenticule-science/attention-allocation-readout-service), a small but real scientific/executable contribution to constrained attention allocation. Its upstream Obsmetrologia research is broader than what this public repository proves.

A fundable next demonstration would let another developer run the readout, compare two clearly documented allocation policies on a bounded problem, inspect their costs and failure cases, and improve an openly reusable result. It would have an explicit experiment, reproducible materials, and a separate real receiving test.

**Who might care?** Engineers building long-lived agents, open scientific-instrument supporters, research patrons interested in collective intelligence.

### Commerce that doesn't have to lock its creators in

A storefront should be something you can inspect, move, repair, and run for your own goods. A marketplace should not quietly steal the seller's merchant authority.

[Read Red Cup's open digital-goods storefront](https://github.com/red-cup-engineering/digital-goods-storefront-services-section) and [Causeway's consuming Shop source](https://github.com/causeway-services/shop.561.group). They include real catalog, payment, delivery and provenance semantics, with explicitly different levels of installation and acceptance.

A fundable demonstration would show **one independent seller using one portable storefront**, with independently evidenced customer browsing, lawful checkout, delivery, recovery, fees and an agent acting only within a real buyer's delegated rights. The real benchmark is the work a creator can accomplish, not a deck announcing parity with Etsy.

**Who might care?** Digital creator patrons, open-commerce maintainers, people who want software that doesn't extract a permanent platform rent.

### Verifiable software claims in an ocean of generated noise

How do we tell whether a claim about a technical result has any inspectable source behind it?

[Red Cup's proof-path structuration service](https://github.com/red-cup-engineering/proof-path-structuration-service) constructs paths from explicit generator evidence and refuses unearned paths. Its source is not an automatic fact-checker or a cryptographic signature of truth: it is a useful foundational piece.

An open follow-on could provide a minimal, reproducible hacker demonstration in which a public claim links to source, provenance, verification steps and a precise refusal when something is missing. This is particularly interesting where generated content is becoming cheap and source examination is becoming valuable.

**Who might care?** Security researchers, hackers, evidence/provenance communities, serious funders of open software.

### A public social web with owners, not hostages

What if a publisher could prepare and inspect a post without surrendering its account keys or editorial judgment?

[Red Cup's social-publication service](https://github.com/red-cup-engineering/social-publication-services-section) publishes the code for source-addressed publication intents and separate credential-blind transport adapters. That is a small step toward a public web where one party's convenience does not become someone else's involuntary agency.

A fundable demonstration would couple independently authorized publication with verifiable post receipts, editable source, opt-out and withdrawal behavior, and compatibility tests on more than one actual social channel. We would measure the *real independent publisher's job*, not simply count generated tweets.

**Who might care?** Public-interest media patrons, FOSS social systems developers, federation and communications maintainers.

## Four different ways to be unusually useful

**Be a reader or an introducer.** This costs nothing. Choose a repository, show us something we missed, or send it to one person with deep technical interest. A credible introduction to someone who might use, challenge or fund it can change the work's trajectory.

**Send a surprise tip.** We already publish free work. If you simply like that fact, use the [live customer-chosen one-time Stripe checkout](https://buy.stripe.com/9B68wQ9HA50ub2Yen25EY04) (currently $5–$10,000 with $250 suggested, received by the emsenn merchant). You can also [sustain work for $10/month](https://buy.stripe.com/28E28s1b4gJcb2Y3Io5EY02). The variable checkout's maximum is a processor product limit, **not** our idea of how generous someone is allowed to be.

**Underwrite a demonstrable piece of open work.** Have a much larger amount or a particular frontier in mind? [Contact the Group](https://561.group/contact/) about your actual interests, proposed amount, public benefit, privacy preference, and whether you want unrestricted support or a separately agreed project. We can talk through a source-backed program and explicitly agreed milestones, rights and funds custody without presuming the sponsor controls research results. A sponsor might be an individual, company, family office, foundation, research organization, or another collective.

**Buy something practical.** If you need to use our capacity instead of merely backing it, that's a real commercial exchange: [ask for a scoped research/engineering/operations/public-service engagement](https://561.group/contact/), or explore [the Shop](https://shop.561.group/). Paid consulting, sponsorship and donations do not have to pretend to be the same transaction.

The suggestion is not that a funder must start at any tier, have a certain wealth profile, or pass a cultural membership test. This is *an invitation to a good match*, not a price list for buying prestige.

## What patrons should expect

You can reasonably expect to inspect the original public artifacts, their licenses, an honest account of actual progress, and the chance to challenge something we say. Where a specific engagement is agreed, its terms should identify work, recipient, evidence of completion, actual payee and remedies. Anonymous recognition or public acknowledgment can be discussed; neither is assumed.

You **cannot** buy our conclusions, hidden equity, special rights to somebody else's culture or records, or an invented promise that a project will succeed. Each public repository has its own license; Indigenous cultural content is not automatically openly licensed.

We are not currently representing this invitation as a 501(c)(3) charitable contribution. The existing Stripe tips and sustaining support are received by **emsenn**, the legal merchant identified at checkout. A separately contracted sponsorship, institutional grant, fiscal-hosted project, or business invoice would have its own correct recipient and transaction terms.

## A different kind of founder story

Someone else might spend $100,000 to hear that an AI company is valued at $10 billion.

You could spend an afternoon inspecting the actual state of public research and deciding that some tiny part of it deserves more existence.

Maybe you'll send $25. Maybe $250,000. Maybe your most useful gift is a demanding introduction to an expert who will tell us exactly where the mathematics, software, engineering, or business needs repairing.

All of those are possible. What we are trying to make less possible is a future in which the only work with funding is work whose creators promised to make it scarce.

**Find a thread. Pull it. Help us make more work that can be pulled apart.**
