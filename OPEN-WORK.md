# The future doesn't need another cap table

*An invitation to the people who enjoy making interesting things possible.*

Suppose an eccentric research group published tools for agent coordination, mathematical verification, and human/agent commerce. Suppose you could inspect the actual source, challenge its claims, keep a copy, and reuse it without investing in the group.

What if **the work was the pitch deck**?

We're the [561 Group](https://github.com/561-group): Lenticule Science, Red Cup Engineering, Coesite Operations, and Causeway Services. We investigate ideas, write software, test it, and try to make it available in forms people can actually use.

We aren't asking you to believe a trillion-dollar valuation. We'd much rather you find an intriguing repository and open it.

## Four rabbit holes, with working source links

### 1. How do you ask software whether an operation preserves what matters?

[Lenticule Science's Hoare Program Carrier](https://github.com/lenticule-science/hoare-program-carrier) is a public, MIT-licensed Lean project that carries formal preservation witnesses and a least-compatible-state result for a particular kind of transition. Its README explains which results are axiom-free and which rely on the library's imported equality reasoning. This is inspectable mathematics, not a promise that we've formally verified all software.

**For:** theorem provers, programming-language researchers, people who enjoy proofs stranger than a pitch deck.

### 2. What happens when an autonomous worker has to choose where limited attention goes?

The public [attention-allocation readout](https://github.com/lenticule-science/attention-allocation-readout-service) is a scientific software boundary for a budget-constrained allocation rule. It is a small, exact piece of a much larger question: how to make resource allocation by agents measurable rather than mystical.

**For:** systems researchers, AI infrastructure builders, anyone funding practical scientific instruments for decision-making.

### 3. Can commerce itself be free software?

[Red Cup's digital-goods storefront components](https://github.com/red-cup-engineering/digital-goods-storefront-services-section) have public source for a shop, catalog, checkout and signed-delivery semantics; [Causeway's actual Shop source](https://github.com/causeway-services/shop.561.group) consumes that supplier. Not every proposed payment, agent or fulfillment path is live, and the source doesn't pretend otherwise. The useful thing is that its contracts and tests are there to inspect and improve.

**For:** open-commerce funders, independent makers, public infrastructure maintainers, creators who want tools they can change.

### 4. What if a social publisher didn't need to own your identity or your credentials?

[Red Cup's social-publication service](https://github.com/red-cup-engineering/social-publication-services-section) contains credential-blind publisher adapters with source-addressed publication intents and receipts, including Bluesky, LinkedIn and Facebook Pages. The receiving organization controls its credentials and editorial authority; a compiled publication intent alone cannot authorize a post.

**For:** independent media, the federated web, public-interest software, builders who want composability without surrendering control.

These are **examples of published work**, not claims that every 561 Group project is public, finished, or open-licensed. Each repository's own LICENSE, documentation, upstream dependencies and governance control what can be reused. Indigenous cultural data is not automatically openly licensed.

**Want to fund a frontier rather than buy equity?** [Explore four concrete open-work underwriting opportunities](UNDERWRITE-OPEN-WORK.md), from research instruments to creator-controlled commerce and independent publishing. You can support any amount, make an introduction, or discuss a substantial unrestricted gift or scoped sponsorship.

## Ways to be part of this

You don't need to buy the future from us to help it exist.

- **Read, test, remix, or critique the source.** A reproducible bug report, an issue that shows a real unmet need, or a qualified pull request can be more valuable than applause.
- **Introduce us to someone who would care.** A researcher, maintainer, funder, technical executive, journalist or procurement office. Tell them *which project* relates to their real interest.
- **Send appreciation for the work already published.** [Choose a one-time tip](https://buy.stripe.com/9B68wQ9HA50ub2Yen25EY04) — $5 to $10,000 through our current customer-selected checkout, with $250 suggested. [Prefer $10/month sustaining support?](https://buy.stripe.com/28E28s1b4gJcb2Y3Io5EY02)
- **Want to underwrite a bigger effort, sponsor a public demonstration, or buy a service?** [Contact the Group](https://561.group/contact/) with the actual outcome or interest and the contribution arrangement you have in mind. Custom contracts and major payments are handled separately, with explicit terms and lawful payees. No pledge amount is assumed.

The live Stripe merchant for these appreciation and monthly-support links is **emsenn**, which is distinct from a claim that an independently incorporated 561 Group legal entity is the seller. A tip buys no equity, financial return, new deliverable, special access, editorial direction, or tax-deductible charitable gift. Monthly support renews until canceled. Open-source licenses and scientific judgment remain independent of patrons.

## For the patron who just wants to see what happens

Maybe you're a serious open-source funder. Maybe you're an engineer with a successful exit, or simply someone delighted that a small research organization spends its time developing things this peculiar. Those are different motivations, and there's room for all of them.

You can support open work without enclosing it. You can help independent researchers continue publishing without purchasing their conclusions. You can tell someone about a problem nobody else is taking seriously.

Our strongest pitch is a rather inconvenient one for the usual startup mythology:

**You don't have to own something for it to be worth making possible.**

If that sounds like your kind of optimism, pick a repository. Find an idea. Start there.
